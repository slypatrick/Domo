# 11 — Board review, investment readiness, year-two options

## 1. Investment thesis

Cash on delivery dominates Tunisian online buying and a large share of parcels are refused (press estimates range from 15–25% in fashion to about 30% overall; sources in 02). Every refused parcel costs the seller two courier legs. The sellers who suffer most run their business from Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp and are not served by store builders. A tool that sits between the seller's chat and the courier, costs almost nothing to run (97% subscription margin in the model), and proves its value in dinars could become the default order desk for this segment, and later the neutral layer through which couriers receive clean, confirmed parcels.

The thesis is unproven. Domo has a working product and no users.

## 2. Why it might fail

| Failure mode | Assessment | Early signal |
|---|---|---|
| Insufficient differentiation | Confirmation can be copied by store builders and couriers | A competitor ships one-tap confirmation |
| Unwillingness to pay | Biggest risk; sellers are used to free tools | PX-0 result |
| Free-user cost | Low in money (0.15 DT each), real in support time | Support hours |
| Weak retention | Double entry with couriers; habit not formed | Week-4 retention |
| Marketplace cold start | Avoided by not investing | — |
| Delivery complexity | Avoided by freezing own delivery | — |
| Margin erosion | Boost at 40% and any messaging automation | Gross margin <60% |
| False trust signals | Reduced by own-shop default and contest flow | Contests upheld |
| Regulation | Data-protection declaration, pending e-commerce bill, e-invoicing | Counsel's opinion |
| Competition | Converty, TikTak Pro, eGrow, couriers' tools | Feature announcements |
| Founder bandwidth | One person for product, sales, support | Missed gates |
| Capital | Opening cash unknown; plan needs about 15,000 DT | Cash below 3 months of fixed costs |
| Feature sprawl | Already happened once | Any batch outside the roadmap |

## 3. Moat: what is realistic

| Possible moat | Realistic in 12 months? | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Distribution | Partly | Referral loops among sellers; courier channel if a partner signs |
| Workflow integration | Yes | Daily pipeline + courier files + team seats |
| Repeat usage | Yes, if retention gates are met | — |
| Partner relationships | Possible | First neutral integration with couriers |
| Operational data | Later | Refusal causes by area and category become valuable with scale; storing data is not a moat by itself |
| Brand trust | Slowly | Depends on fairness to buyers |
| Legitimate switching costs | Moderate | History, team habits, integrations; data stays exportable |
| Network effects | Not in year one | Cross-shop history is the only true network effect and is legally gated |

Honest conclusion: no moat today. The first defensible position is being the tool sellers open every morning, connected to their courier.

## 4. Milestones

| Milestone | Definition |
|---|---|
| Product validation | 10 of 20 pilot sellers activated; median time to first link <3 minutes |
| Initial commercial traction | 20 paying merchants, payment success ≥90% |
| Repeatable acquisition | One channel delivering ≥15 new paying merchants a month at CAC <200 DT for three months |
| Early product-market fit evidence | Week-12 retention of activated sellers ≥50%; paid churn ≤5% for three months; ≥30% of new signups from referrals; ≥40% of sellers "very disappointed" if Domo disappeared (survey of ≥30 users) |
| Financial viability | Three consecutive months of positive operating result with a founder draw |
| Ready to seek investment | The four above plus MRR ≥5,000 DT and a signed courier partner |
| Ready to expand | Unit economics measured on ≥3 paid cohorts; support under control with a contractor |

## 5. Board-level view of the plan

- **Ask of the founder:** 30–35 hours a week, about 15,000 DT over the year, and the discipline to keep four product lines frozen.
- **What the year buys:** a clear answer to "will social sellers pay for confirmation?" by the end of February, and, if yes, about 44 paying merchants and a business near monthly break-even by November (base case).
- **What it does not buy:** scale. Subscription revenue alone in the mid-case serviceable market is about 0.56 M DT a year at full potential (02 §2).
- **When not to spend:** no paid ads before activation ≥25%; no hire before 150 active merchants; no courier API before a signed partner; nothing on the marketplace or own delivery in year one.

## 6. Investment readiness

Not ready today: no entity confirmed, no revenue, no cohorts. Fundable story after G2 if met: measured activation, conversion and retention on real cohorts, a courier partner in discussion, and a use of funds tied to the funded roadmap (part-time developer, support, acquisition). Suitable sources at that stage (prospects to research, none contacted): local incubators and accelerators, startup-label programmes, angel investors with e-commerce or logistics background. Prepare: one-page monthly review from March, cohort charts, the workbook with actuals replacing assumptions.

## 7. Year-two options (to choose at gate G4)

| Option | Precondition | Upside | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Courier network layer: booking with several couriers, outcome-based comparison, referral fees | One partner live, ≥70% of orders with outcomes | Revenue per order without billing sellers | Partner dependence |
| B. High-volume teams: call-centre style confirmation queue, roles, SLA | ≥10 Business customers asking | Higher ARPU (sur mesure) | Support load |
| C. Confirmation API for store platforms | ≥10 retained API users | Distribution through platforms | Platforms build it themselves |
| D. Prepayment and deposits through a licensed gateway (the gateway holds funds) | Legal opinion; gateway partner | Attacks refusals at the root; aligns with public policy | Buyer adoption |
| E. Shared trust signals with lawful basis | Counsel and INPDP position; buyer notice and contest proven | Network effect | Legal and reputational |
| F. Marketplace discovery | ≥300 active stores with catalogues | Buyer-side brand | Cold start, capital |
| G. Regional expansion (Algeria, Morocco, Libya: COD-heavy markets, INFERRED) | Repeatable acquisition at home | Larger market | Local competitors, founder bandwidth |
| H. Own delivery | Conditions in decision D2 | Margin per parcel | Capital, liability |

Suggested order to study: A, D, B, C; E only with legal clearance; F, G, H last.
